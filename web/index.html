    Is the dark web dangerous?

    Not inherently — the dark web is just a set of sites reachable only through Tor, and Tor itself is a legitimate privacy tool used by journalists, whistleblowers, and anyone who doesn't want their browsing tied to their identity. The risk isn't the network, it's specific content on it: marketplaces selling stolen goods, leak sites hosting real people's stolen data, and no shortage of straightforward scams preying on the assumption that "dark web" means "no rules, so no recourse."

    The practical risk to you as a visitor is mostly the same as anywhere online — phishing pages, malware disguised as a download, and social engineering — just with less accountability for whoever's running the site. That's what the tools and ratings on this platform are for.

    How the safety ratings work

    Verified

    A confirmed legitimate service — its operator has proven ownership through this platform's verification process, or it's a known official mirror.

    Unrated

    Crawled, nothing specific flagged. Not the same as "safe" — just "nothing detected yet."

    Suspicious

    Signals worth caution: an illicit marketplace, or a pattern matching a documented exit-scam (a sudden shutdown after a spike in activity).

    Dangerous

    Concrete evidence of harm: a confirmed data leak, fraud content, a link to an executable file, or a near-duplicate of a known legitimate site (a phishing clone).

    These come from automatic classification and pattern detection, not manual review of every site — treat a rating as a strong signal, not a guarantee either way.

    Browsing safely

    • Use Tor Browser, keep it updated, and don't resize its window or install extensions — both make you easier to fingerprint. The Tor Browser checker under Tools gives a quick read on your current setup.
    • Never download or run a file from a site you don't already trust. If you must inspect one, check the URL first with the tools here.
    • Never enter a real password, email, or payment detail on a site reached from an unfamiliar link — check it first, the same way you would a suspicious email.
    • Treat "Dangerous" and "Suspicious" ratings as a real stop sign, not a suggestion — the reveal-gate on search results exists so you see the rating before the address, not after.
    • If something looks like a scam, it usually is — see the pattern gallery below for what the common ones actually look like.

    Scam pattern gallery

    Illustrative examples of the categories the scam-checker tools recognize — real scams vary in wording, but the underlying pattern repeats. Recognizing the pattern matters more than any specific phrase.

    Phishing

    "We noticed unusual activity on your account. Verify your identity within 24 hours or it will be suspended."
    • Urgency + a login link
    • Generic greeting, no your real name
    • Domain that's close to, but not, the real one

    Romance scam

    "I feel so close to you already. I just need help covering a customs fee to finally come see you."
    • Fast escalation of intimacy
    • Always has a reason not to video call
    • Money request tied to travel or an emergency

    Crypto / investment scam

    "Our algorithm guarantees 40% weekly returns. Early investors are already cashing out — spots close soon."
    • Guaranteed returns (no legitimate investment guarantees this)
    • Artificial urgency
    • Requires an upfront deposit to a wallet you don't control

    Fake delivery

    "Your package could not be delivered. Pay a $2.99 redelivery fee within 48 hours."
    • Small fee (designed to seem not worth questioning)
    • Link instead of a real tracking number
    • You weren't expecting a package

    Tech support scam

    "WARNING: Your device has 3 viruses. Call this number immediately or your data will be lost."
    • A pop-up or page you can't easily close
    • Pushes a phone call, not a real support channel
    • Asks you to install remote-access software

    Job scam

    "Earn $500/day working from home. To get started, purchase your starter kit for $49."
    • Payment required before any real work
    • Vague job description
    • Asks you to reship packages or move money through your own account

    Lottery / inheritance scam

    "You have been selected to receive $850,000 from an unclaimed estate. A small processing fee is required to release funds."
    • You never entered anything
    • A fee required to receive money you supposedly already won
    • Pressure to act before someone else claims it
